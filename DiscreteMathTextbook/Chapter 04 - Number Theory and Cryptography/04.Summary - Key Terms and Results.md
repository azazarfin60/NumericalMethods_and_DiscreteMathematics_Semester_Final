# Chapter 4 — Key Terms and Results

## Key Terms

### Section 4.1: Divisibility and Modular Arithmetic
*See [[04.1 Divisibility and Modular Arithmetic]]*

- **$a \mid b$ ($a$ divides $b$):** there is an integer $c$ such that $b = ac$.
- **divisor / factor:** when $a \mid b$, $a$ is called a divisor or factor of $b$.
- **multiple:** when $a \mid b$, $b$ is called a multiple of $a$.
- **$a \text{ div } d$:** the quotient obtained when $a$ is divided by the positive integer $d$, equal to $\lfloor a/d \rfloor$.
- **$a \bmod d$:** the remainder obtained when $a$ is divided by the positive integer $d$, equal to $a - d\lfloor a/d \rfloor$.
- **$a \equiv b \pmod m$ ($a$ is congruent to $b$ modulo $m$):** $a - b$ is divisible by $m$.
- **congruence:** a statement $a \equiv b \pmod m$.
- **modulus:** the integer $m$ in the congruence $a \equiv b \pmod m$.
- **congruence class:** the set of all integers congruent to an integer $a$ modulo $m$.
- **modular arithmetic:** arithmetic operations $+_m$ and $\cdot_m$ defined on $\mathbf{Z}_m = \{0, 1, \dots, m - 1\}$.

### Section 4.2: Integer Representations and Algorithms
*See [[04.2 Integer Representations and Algorithms]]*

- **base $b$ representation:** the unique representation of a positive integer $n$ in the form $n = a_k b^k + a_{k-1} b^{k-1} + \dots + a_1 b + a_0$, denoted $(a_k a_{k-1} \dots a_1 a_0)_b$.
- **binary representation:** the base 2 representation of an integer.
- **octal representation:** the base 8 representation of an integer.
- **hexadecimal representation:** the base 16 representation of an integer.
- **modular exponentiation:** computing $b^n \bmod m$ efficiently using the binary expansion of $n$.

---

### Section 4.3: Primes and Greatest Common Divisors
*See [[04.3 Primes and Greatest Common Divisors]]*

- **prime:** an integer greater than 1 with exactly two positive integer divisors (1 and itself).
- **composite:** an integer greater than 1 that is not prime.
- **trial division:** a brute-force method to test primality or find prime factors by dividing by primes up to $\sqrt{n}$.
- **sieve of Eratosthenes:** a systematic procedure for finding all primes not exceeding a specified positive integer $n$.
- **Mersenne prime:** a prime of the form $2^p - 1$, where $p$ is prime.
- **$\gcd(a, b)$ (greatest common divisor):** the largest integer that divides both $a$ and $b$.
- **relatively prime integers:** integers $a$ and $b$ such that $\gcd(a, b) = 1$.
- **pairwise relatively prime integers:** a set of integers where every pair of distinct integers is relatively prime.
- **$\text{lcm}(a, b)$ (least common multiple):** the smallest positive integer that is divisible by both $a$ and $b$.
- **linear combination of $a$ and $b$ with integer coefficients:** an expression of the form $sa + tb$, where $s$ and $t$ are integers.
- **Bézout coefficients of $a$ and $b$:** integers $s$ and $t$ such that the Bézout identity $sa + tb = \gcd(a, b)$ holds.
- **Bézout's identity:** the equation $sa + tb = \gcd(a, b)$.

### Section 4.4: Solving Congruences
*See [[04.4 Solving Congruences]]*

- **linear congruence:** a congruence of the form $ax \equiv b \pmod m$, where $x$ is an integer variable.
- **inverse of $a$ modulo $m$:** an integer $\overline{a}$ such that $\overline{a}a \equiv 1 \pmod m$.
- **Chinese remainder theorem:** a method to find a unique simultaneous solution modulo the product of pairwise relatively prime moduli.
- **back substitution:** a method for solving systems of linear congruences step-by-step.
- **pseudoprime to the base $b$:** a composite positive integer $n$ such that $b^{n-1} \equiv 1 \pmod n$.
- **Carmichael number:** a composite integer $n$ that satisfies $b^{n-1} \equiv 1 \pmod n$ for all positive integers $b$ with $\gcd(b, n) = 1$.
- **primitive root of a prime $p$:** an integer $r \in \mathbf{Z}_p$ such that every nonzero element of $\mathbf{Z}_p$ is a power of $r$.
- **discrete logarithm:** the integer $e$ with $0 \leq e \leq p - 1$ such that $r^e \equiv a \pmod p$, denoted $\log_r a = e$.

### Section 4.5: Applications of Congruences
*See [[04.5 Applications of Congruences]]*

- **hashing function:** a function $h(k) = k \bmod m$ assigning memory location $h(k)$ to a key $k$.
- **collision:** when a hashing function assigns more than one file or key to the same memory location.
- **pseudorandom numbers:** numbers generated by deterministic algorithms that exhibit properties of random numbers.
- **linear congruential method:** recursive generation of pseudorandom numbers via $x_{n+1} = (a x_n + c) \bmod m$.
- **pure multiplicative generator:** a linear congruential generator with increment $c = 0$.
- **check digit:** an extra digit appended to an identification string to detect transmission or transcription errors.
- **parity check bit:** an extra bit appended to a bit string so that the total sum of bits mod 2 is 0 or 1.
- **transposition error:** an error occurring when two adjacent or separated digits are accidentally interchanged.

### Section 4.6: Cryptography
*See [[04.6 Cryptography]]*

- **cryptography:** the study of methods for encrypting and decrypting secret messages.
- **encryption:** the process of transforming a plaintext message into ciphertext to make it secret.
- **decryption:** the process of recovering the original plaintext from the ciphertext.
- **encryption key:** a parameter that determines which specific encryption transformation from a family of transformations is used.
- **shift cipher:** a cipher that encrypts the plaintext letter $p$ as $(p + k) \bmod 26$ for an integer key $k$.
- **affine cipher:** a cipher that encrypts the plaintext letter $p$ as $(ap + b) \bmod 26$ where $\gcd(a, 26) = 1$.
- **monoalphabetic / character cipher:** a cipher that encrypts individual characters one at a time.
- **block cipher:** a cipher that transforms blocks of characters into blocks of characters.
- **transposition cipher:** a block cipher that encrypts a block by permuting its positions using a permutation $\sigma$.
- **cryptanalysis:** the process of recovering plaintext from ciphertext without knowledge of the key or encryption algorithm.
- **cryptosystem:** a five-tuple $(P, C, K, E, D)$ consisting of plaintext strings $P$, ciphertext strings $C$, keyspace $K$, encryption functions $E$, and decryption functions $D$.
- **private key encryption:** cryptosystems where both encryption and decryption keys must be kept secret.
- **public key encryption:** cryptosystems where encryption keys are publicly available, but decryption keys are secret.
- **RSA cryptosystem:** a public key cryptosystem where $n = pq$, $e$ is coprime to $(p - 1)(q - 1)$, encryption is $C = M^e \bmod n$, and decryption is $M = C^d \bmod n$ with $de \equiv 1 \pmod{(p - 1)(q - 1)}$.
- **key exchange protocol:** a protocol (such as Diffie-Hellman) enabling two parties to establish a shared secret key over an insecure channel.
- **digital signature:** a mechanism (e.g., using RSA decryption with a sender's private key) that allows a recipient to verify the authenticity and sender of a message.

## Key Results

- **The Division Algorithm:** Let $a$ be an integer and $d$ a positive integer. Then there are unique integers $q$ and $r$ with $0 \leq r < d$ such that $a = dq + r$.
- **Divisibility Properties:** If $a \mid b$ and $a \mid c$, then $a \mid (b + c)$; if $a \mid b$, then $a \mid bc$; if $a \mid b$ and $b \mid c$, then $a \mid c$.
- **Linear Combination Divisibility:** If $a \mid b$ and $a \mid c$, then $a \mid (mb + nc)$ for all integers $m, n$.
- **Base $b$ Representation Theorem:** Let $b > 1$ be an integer. Then every positive integer $n$ can be uniquely expressed in base $b$ as $n = a_k b^k + a_{k-1} b^{k-1} + \dots + a_1 b + a_0$.
- **The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic:** Every integer greater than 1 can be written uniquely as a prime or as the product of two or more primes in nondecreasing order.
- **Prime Divisor Bound:** If $n$ is a composite integer, then $n$ has a prime divisor less than or equal to $\sqrt{n}$.
- **Infinitude of Primes:** There are infinitely many prime numbers.
- **The Prime Number Theorem:** The ratio of the number of primes not exceeding $x$ and $x/\ln x$ approaches 1 as $x \to \infty$.
- **GCD and LCM Formulae via Prime Factorizations:**
  $$\gcd(a, b) = \prod p_i^{\min(a_i, b_i)}, \quad \text{lcm}(a, b) = \prod p_i^{\max(a_i, b_i)}.$$
- **Product Identity:** For positive integers $a$ and $b$, $ab = \gcd(a, b) \cdot \text{lcm}(a, b)$.
- **Euclidean Algorithm Property:** If $a = bq + r$, then $\gcd(a, b) = \gcd(b, r)$.
- **Bézout's Theorem:** If $a$ and $b$ are positive integers, then there exist integers $s$ and $t$ such that $\gcd(a, b) = sa + tb$.
- **Euclid's Lemma:** If $a, b, c$ are positive integers with $\gcd(a, b) = 1$ and $a \mid bc$, then $a \mid c$. If $p$ is prime and $p \mid a_1 a_2 \dots a_n$, then $p \mid a_i$ for some $i$.
- **Modular Division Cancellation:** If $ac \equiv bc \pmod m$ and $\gcd(c, m) = 1$, then $a \equiv b \pmod m$.
- **Modular Inverse Existence:** If $\gcd(a, m) = 1$ and $m > 1$, then $a$ has a unique inverse modulo $m$.
- **The Chinese Remainder Theorem:** A system of linear congruences $x \equiv a_i \pmod{m_i}$ with pairwise relatively prime moduli $m_1, \dots, m_n > 1$ has a unique solution modulo $m = m_1 m_2 \dots m_n$.
- **Fermat's Little Theorem:** If $p$ is prime and $p \nmid a$, then $a^{p-1} \equiv 1 \pmod p$, and for every integer $a$, $a^p \equiv a \pmod p$.
