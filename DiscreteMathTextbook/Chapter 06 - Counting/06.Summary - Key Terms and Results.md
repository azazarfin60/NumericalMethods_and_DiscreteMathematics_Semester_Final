# Chapter 6 — Key Terms and Results

## Key Terms

### Section 6.1: The Basics of Counting
*See [[06.1 The Basics of Counting]]*

- **combinatorics:** the study of arrangements of objects.
- **enumeration:** the counting of arrangements of objects.
- **product rule:** a principle stating that if a procedure can be broken down into two successive tasks with $n_1$ and $n_2$ ways, there are $n_1n_2$ ways to do the procedure.
- **sum rule:** a principle stating that if a task can be done in one of $n_1$ ways or one of $n_2$ ways, where the sets of ways are disjoint, there are $n_1 + n_2$ ways to do the task.
- **subtraction rule (inclusion–exclusion for two sets):** if a task can be done in either $n_1$ ways or $n_2$ ways, the number of ways is $n_1 + n_2$ minus the number of ways common to both.
- **division rule:** there are $n/d$ ways to do a task if it can be done in $n$ ways and for every way $w$, exactly $d$ of the $n$ ways correspond to $w$.
- **tree diagram:** a diagram made up of a root, branches leaving the root, and other branches leaving some of the endpoints of branches, used to model sequences of choices.

---

### Section 6.2: The Pigeonhole Principle
*See [[06.2 The Pigeonhole Principle]]*

- **the pigeonhole principle (Dirichlet drawer principle):** when more than $k$ objects are placed in $k$ boxes, there must be a box containing more than one object.
- **the generalized pigeonhole principle:** when $N$ objects are placed in $k$ boxes, there must be a box containing at least $\lceil N/k \rceil$ objects.
- **subsequence:** a sequence obtained from an original sequence by selecting terms in their original order.
- **strictly increasing sequence:** a sequence where each term is strictly larger than the term preceding it.
- **strictly decreasing sequence:** a sequence where each term is strictly smaller than the term preceding it.
- **Ramsey theory:** a branch of combinatorics dealing with the distribution of subsets of elements of sets, guaranteeing order within sufficiently large structures.
- **Ramsey number $R(m, n)$:** the minimum number of people at a party such that there are either $m$ mutual friends or $n$ mutual enemies, assuming each pair are friends or enemies.

---

### Section 6.3: Permutations and Combinations
*See [[06.3 Permutations and Combinations]]*

- **permutation:** an ordered arrangement of the elements of a set.
- **$r$-permutation:** an ordered arrangement of $r$ elements of a set.
- **$P(n, r)$:** the number of $r$-permutations of a set with $n$ elements.
- **$r$-combination:** an unordered selection of $r$ elements of a set (a subset of size $r$).
- **$C(n, r)$ / $\binom{n}{r}$:** the number of $r$-combinations of a set with $n$ elements.
- **binomial coefficient $\binom{n}{r}$:** the coefficient of $x^{n-r}y^r$ in the expansion of $(x + y)^n$, equal to $C(n, r)$.
- **combinatorial proof:** a proof that uses counting arguments rather than algebraic manipulation to establish an identity.
- **double counting proof:** a combinatorial proof showing that two expressions count the same set of objects in two different ways.
- **bijective proof:** a combinatorial proof establishing a one-to-one correspondence (bijection) between two sets.

---

### Section 6.4: Binomial Coefficients and Identities
*See [[06.4 Binomial Coefficients and Identities]]*

- **the binomial theorem:** formula expressing the power of a binomial expression $(x + y)^n$ as a sum of terms involving binomial coefficients.
- **Pascal’s identity:** the recurrence relation $\binom{n+1}{k} = \binom{n}{k-1} + \binom{n}{k}$.
- **Pascal’s triangle:** a triangular arrangement of binomial coefficients where each interior entry is the sum of the two entries directly above it.
- **Vandermonde’s identity:** the convolution formula $\binom{m+n}{r} = \sum_{k=0}^r \binom{m}{r-k}\binom{n}{k}$.

---

### Section 6.5: Generalized Permutations and Combinations
*See [[06.5 Generalized Permutations and Combinations]]*

- **permutation with repetition:** an ordered sequence of length $r$ chosen from $n$ elements where elements may be reused ($n^r$).
- **combination with repetition ($r$-combination with repetition):** an unordered selection of $r$ elements from $n$ elements with replacement, modeled by stars and bars ($C(n + r - 1, r)$).
- **permutations with indistinguishable objects:** arrangements of $n$ objects containing duplicate types.
- **Stirling number of the second kind $S(n, j)$:** the number of ways to distribute $n$ distinguishable objects into $j$ indistinguishable boxes so that no box is empty.
- **partition of a positive integer:** a representation of $n$ as a sum of positive integers written in nonincreasing order.

---

### Section 6.6: Generating Permutations and Combinations
*See [[06.6 Generating Permutations and Combinations]]*

- **lexicographic order:** dictionary ordering applied to sequences, permutations, bit strings, and combinations.

## Key Results

- **The Product Rule for Counting:** If a procedure can be broken down into a sequence of two tasks, with $n_1$ ways for the first and $n_2$ ways for the second, there are $n_1n_2$ ways to carry out the procedure.
  - In terms of sets: $|A_1 \times A_2 \times \dots \times A_m| = |A_1| \cdot |A_2| \cdots |A_m|$.

- **The Sum Rule for Counting:** If a task can be done in one of $n_1$ ways or one of $n_2$ ways (mutually exclusive), there are $n_1 + n_2$ ways to do the task.
  - In terms of sets: $|A_1 \cup A_2 \cup \dots \cup A_m| = |A_1| + |A_2| + \dots + |A_m|$ when the sets $A_i$ are pairwise disjoint.

- **The Subtraction Rule (Principle of Inclusion–Exclusion):** If a task can be done in either $n_1$ ways or $n_2$ ways:
  $$\text{Total ways} = n_1 + n_2 - (\text{ways common to both})$$
  - In terms of sets: $|A_1 \cup A_2| = |A_1| + |A_2| - |A_1 \cap A_2|$.

- **The Division Rule:** There are $n/d$ ways to do a task if it can be done in $n$ ways and for every way $w$, exactly $d$ of the $n$ ways correspond to $w$.
  - In terms of sets: If finite set $A$ is the union of $n$ pairwise disjoint subsets each of size $d$, then $n = |A|/d$.
  - In terms of functions: If $f: A \to B$ is $d$-to-one, then $|B| = |A|/d$.

- **The Pigeonhole Principle:** If $k$ is a positive integer and $k + 1$ or more objects are placed into $k$ boxes, then there is at least one box containing two or more of the objects.
  - In terms of functions: A function from a set with $k + 1$ or more elements to a set with $k$ elements is not one-to-one.

- **The Generalized Pigeonhole Principle:** If $N$ objects are placed into $k$ boxes, then there is at least one box containing at least $\lceil N/k \rceil$ objects.
  - Minimum number of objects to guarantee at least $r$ in one of $k$ boxes: $N = k(r - 1) + 1$.

- **Monotone Subsequences (Theorem 3 of Section 6.2):** Every sequence of $n^2 + 1$ distinct real numbers contains a subsequence of length $n + 1$ that is either strictly increasing or strictly decreasing.

- **Ramsey Numbers:** $R(3, 3) = 6$, $R(m, n) = R(n, m)$, and $R(2, n) = n$ for $n \ge 2$.

- **Number of $r$-Permutations:**
  $$P(n, r) = n(n - 1)(n - 2) \cdots (n - r + 1) = \frac{n!}{(n - r)!}$$

- **Number of $r$-Combinations:**
  $$C(n, r) = \binom{n}{r} = \frac{n!}{r!(n - r)!} = \frac{n(n - 1) \cdots (n - r + 1)}{r!}$$

- **Symmetry of Combinations:**
  $$C(n, r) = C(n, n - r)$$

- **The Binomial Theorem:**
  $$(x + y)^n = \sum_{j=0}^n \binom{n}{j} x^{n-j}y^j = \sum_{k=0}^n \binom{n}{k} x^{n-k}y^k$$

- **Binomial Identities:**
  - $\sum_{k=0}^n \binom{n}{k} = 2^n$
  - $\sum_{k=0}^n (-1)^k \binom{n}{k} = 0 \implies \binom{n}{0} + \binom{n}{2} + \binom{n}{4} + \dots = \binom{n}{1} + \binom{n}{3} + \dots$
  - $\sum_{k=0}^n 2^k \binom{n}{k} = 3^n$
  - Pascal’s Identity: $\binom{n+1}{k} = \binom{n}{k-1} + \binom{n}{k}$
  - Vandermonde’s Identity: $\binom{m+n}{r} = \sum_{k=0}^r \binom{m}{r-k}\binom{n}{k}$
  - $\binom{2n}{n} = \sum_{k=0}^n \binom{n}{k}^2$
  - $\binom{n+1}{r+1} = \sum_{j=r}^n \binom{j}{r}$

- **Summary of Selection Formulas (Table 1 of Section 6.5):**
  - Order matters, no repetition: $P(n, r) = \frac{n!}{(n - r)!}$
  - Order does not matter, no repetition: $C(n, r) = \frac{n!}{r!(n - r)!}$
  - Order matters, repetition allowed: $n^r$
  - Order does not matter, repetition allowed: $C(n + r - 1, r) = \frac{(n + r - 1)!}{r!(n - 1)!}$

- **Permutations with Indistinguishable Objects:**
  The number of permutations of $n$ objects with $n_i$ indistinguishable objects of type $i$ is:
  $$\frac{n!}{n_1! n_2! \cdots n_k!}$$

- **Distributing Objects into Boxes:**
  - *Distinguishable objects, distinguishable boxes:* $\frac{n!}{n_1! n_2! \cdots n_k!}$ ways to place $n_i$ into box $i$.
  - *Indistinguishable objects, distinguishable boxes:* $C(k + n - 1, n)$ ways to place $n$ objects into $k$ boxes.
  - *Distinguishable objects, indistinguishable boxes:* $\sum_{j=1}^k S(n, j)$ ways, where:
    $$S(n, j) = \frac{1}{j!} \sum_{i=0}^{j-1} (-1)^i \binom{j}{i} (j - i)^n$$
  - *Indistinguishable objects, indistinguishable boxes:* $p_k(n)$ ways (partitions of $n$ into at most $k$ positive integers).

- **Generation Algorithms:**
  - *Algorithm 1:* Generates the next permutation in lexicographic order from $a_1a_2\dots a_n$.
  - *Algorithm 2:* Generates the next larger bit string.
  - *Algorithm 3:* Generates the next $r$-combination in lexicographic order.
