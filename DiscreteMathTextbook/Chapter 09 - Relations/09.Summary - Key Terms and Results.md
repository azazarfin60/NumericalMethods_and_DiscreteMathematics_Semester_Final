# Chapter 9 — Key Terms and Results

## Key Terms

### Section 9.1: Relations and Their Properties
*See [[09.1 Relations and Their Properties]]*

- **binary relation from $A$ to $B$:** a subset of $A \times B$.
- **relation on $A$:** a binary relation from $A$ to itself (i.e., a subset of $A \times A$).
- **$S \circ R$ (composite of $R$ and $S$):** the relation consisting of ordered pairs $(a, c)$ such that there exists an element $b$ where $(a, b) \in R$ and $(b, c) \in S$.
- **$R^{-1}$ (inverse relation of $R$):** the relation $\{(b, a) \mid (a, b) \in R\}$.
- **$R^n$ ($n$th power of $R$):** powers of $R$ defined inductively by $R^1 = R$ and $R^{n+1} = R^n \circ R$.
- **reflexive:** a relation $R$ on $A$ is reflexive if $(a, a) \in R$ for all $a \in A$.
- **symmetric:** a relation $R$ on $A$ is symmetric if $(b, a) \in R$ whenever $(a, b) \in R$.
- **antisymmetric:** a relation $R$ on $A$ is antisymmetric if $a = b$ whenever $(a, b) \in R$ and $(b, a) \in R$.
- **transitive:** a relation $R$ on $A$ is transitive if $(a, b) \in R$ and $(b, c) \in R$ implies that $(a, c) \in R$.

### Section 9.2: $n$-ary Relations and Their Applications
*See [[09.2 n-ary Relations and Their Applications]]*

- **$n$-ary relation on $A_1, A_2, \dots, A_n$:** a subset of $A_1 \times A_2 \times \dots \times A_n$.
- **relational data model:** a model for representing databases using $n$-ary relations.
- **primary key:** a domain of an $n$-ary relation such that an $n$-tuple is uniquely determined by its value for this domain.
- **composite key:** the Cartesian product of domains of an $n$-ary relation such that an $n$-tuple is uniquely determined by its values in these domains.
- **selection operator:** a function that selects the $n$-tuples in an $n$-ary relation that satisfy a specified condition.
- **projection:** a function that produces relations of smaller degree from an $n$-ary relation by deleting fields.
- **join:** a function that combines $n$-ary relations that agree on certain fields.

### Section 9.3: Representing Relations
*See [[09.3 Representing Relations]]*

- **zero–one matrix:** a matrix $\mathbf{M}_R = [m_{ij}]$ representing a relation $R$, where $m_{ij} = 1$ if $(a_i, b_j) \in R$ and $0$ otherwise.
- **directed graph (or digraph):** a set of elements called vertices and ordered pairs of these elements, called edges.
- **loop:** an edge of the form $(a, a)$ that connects a vertex to itself.

---

### Section 9.4: Closures of Relations
*See [[09.4 Closures of Relations]]*

- **closure of a relation $R$ with respect to a property $\mathbf{P}$:** the relation $S$ (if it exists) that contains $R$, has property $\mathbf{P}$, and is contained within any relation that contains $R$ and has property $\mathbf{P}$.
- **reflexive closure:** the smallest reflexive relation containing $R$, equal to $R \cup \Delta$, where $\Delta = \{(a, a) \mid a \in A\}$.
- **symmetric closure:** the smallest symmetric relation containing $R$, equal to $R \cup R^{-1}$.
- **path in a digraph:** a sequence of edges $(x_0, x_1), (x_1, x_2), \dots, (x_{n-1}, x_n)$ such that the terminal vertex of each edge is the initial vertex of the succeeding edge in the sequence.
- **circuit (or cycle) in a digraph:** a path of length at least 1 that begins and ends at the same vertex.
- **$R^*$ (connectivity relation):** the relation consisting of those ordered pairs $(a, b)$ such that there is a path from $a$ to $b$ in $R$.
- **transitive closure:** the smallest transitive relation containing $R$, equal to the connectivity relation $R^* = \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty R^n$.
- **interior vertices:** in a path $a, x_1, x_2, \dots, x_{m-1}, b$, the vertices $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_{m-1}$ other than the endpoints.

### Section 9.5: Equivalence Relations
*See [[09.5 Equivalence Relations]]*

- **equivalence relation:** a relation that is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive.
- **equivalent:** if $R$ is an equivalence relation, $a$ is equivalent to $b$ ($a \sim b$) if $a R b$.
- **$[a]_R$ (equivalence class of $a$ with respect to $R$):** the set of all elements of $A$ that are equivalent to $a$, i.e., $[a]_R = \{s \mid (a, s) \in R\}$.
- **representative:** any element belonging to an equivalence class $[a]_R$.
- **$[a]_m$ (congruence class modulo $m$):** the set of integers congruent to $a$ modulo $m$.
- **partition of a set $S$:** a collection of pairwise disjoint nonempty subsets $\{A_i \mid i \in I\}$ that have $S$ as their union.

### Section 9.6: Partial Orderings
*See [[09.6 Partial Orderings]]*

- **partial ordering (or partial order):** a relation that is reflexive, antisymmetric, and transitive.
- **poset $(S, R)$:** a set $S$ together with a partial ordering $R$ on this set.
- **comparable:** the elements $a$ and $b$ in the poset $(S, \preceq)$ are comparable if $a \preceq b$ or $b \preceq a$.
- **incomparable:** elements $a$ and $b$ in a poset that are not comparable.
- **total (or linear) ordering:** a partial ordering for which every pair of elements is comparable.
- **totally (or linearly) ordered set (chain):** a poset with a total (or linear) ordering.
- **well-ordered set:** a poset $(S, \preceq)$, where $\preceq$ is a total order and every nonempty subset of $S$ has a least element.
- **lexicographic order:** a partial ordering defined on Cartesian products or strings based on the first element/character where they differ.
- **Hasse diagram:** a graphical representation of a finite poset where loops and all edges resulting from the transitive property are removed, and the direction of edges is upward from initial to terminal vertex without arrows.
- **covering relation:** the set of pairs $(x, y)$ such that $y$ covers $x$ (i.e., $x \prec y$ and there is no $z$ with $x \prec z \prec y$).
- **maximal element:** an element of a poset that is not less than any other element of the poset.
- **minimal element:** an element of a poset that is not greater than any other element of the poset.
- **greatest element:** an element $a$ of a poset greater than all other elements in the poset ($b \preceq a$ for all $b \in S$).
- **least element:** an element $a$ of a poset less than all other elements in the poset ($a \preceq b$ for all $b \in S$).
- **upper bound:** an element $u$ of a poset such that $a \preceq u$ for all elements $a \in A$.
- **lower bound:** an element $l$ of a poset such that $l \preceq a$ for all elements $a \in A$.
- **least upper bound ($\text{lub}(A)$):** an upper bound of $A$ that is less than every other upper bound of $A$.
- **greatest lower bound ($\text{glb}(A)$):** a lower bound of $A$ that is greater than every other lower bound of $A$.
- **lattice:** a partially ordered set in which every pair of elements has both a least upper bound and a greatest lower bound.
- **compatible total ordering:** a total ordering that contains the given partial ordering ($a \preceq b$ whenever $a R b$).
- **topological sort:** the construction of a compatible total ordering from a given partial ordering.

## Key Results

### Section 9.1: Relations and Their Properties
*See [[09.1 Relations and Their Properties]]*

- **Number of Relations:** There are $2^{mn}$ relations from a set with $m$ elements to a set with $n$ elements, and $2^{n^2}$ relations on a set with $n$ elements.
- **Characterization of Transitivity:** The relation $R$ on a set $A$ is transitive if and only if $R^n \subseteq R$ for $n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$.

### Section 9.3: Representing Relations
*See [[09.3 Representing Relations]]*

- **Properties from Zero–One Matrices:**
  - $R$ is reflexive if and only if all main diagonal entries $m_{ii} = 1$.
  - $R$ is symmetric if and only if $\mathbf{M}_R = (\mathbf{M}_R)^t$.
  - $R$ is antisymmetric if and only if $m_{ij} = 0$ or $m_{ji} = 0$ for all $i \neq j$.
- **Operations on Relations via Matrices:**
  - $\mathbf{M}_{R_1 \cup R_2} = \mathbf{M}_{R_1} \vee \mathbf{M}_{R_2}$
  - $\mathbf{M}_{R_1 \cap R_2} = \mathbf{M}_{R_1} \wedge \mathbf{M}_{R_2}$
  - $\mathbf{M}_{S \circ R} = \mathbf{M}_R \odot \mathbf{M}_S$

### Section 9.4: Closures of Relations
*See [[09.4 Closures of Relations]]*

- **Reflexive Closure:** The reflexive closure of a relation $R$ on the set $A$ equals $R \cup \Delta$, where $\Delta = \{(a, a) \mid a \in A\}$.
- **Symmetric Closure:** The symmetric closure of a relation $R$ on the set $A$ equals $R \cup R^{-1}$, where $R^{-1} = \{(b, a) \mid (a, b) \in R\}$.
- **Paths and Powers:** There is a path of length $n$ from $a$ to $b$ in $R$ if and only if $(a, b) \in R^n$.
- **Transitive Closure Theorem:** The transitive closure of a relation $R$ equals the connectivity relation $R^* = \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty R^n$.
- **Path Length Bound:** If there is a path from $a$ to $b$ in a poset with $n$ elements, there is such a path with length not exceeding $n$ (and not exceeding $n - 1$ if $a \neq b$).
- **Matrix Formulation of Transitive Closure:** $\mathbf{M}_{R^*} = \mathbf{M}_R \vee \mathbf{M}_R^{[2]} \vee \dots \vee \mathbf{M}_R^{[n]}$.
- **Warshall's Algorithm:** Computes the transitive closure $\mathbf{M}_{R^*}$ using $2n^3$ bit operations by constructing intermediate matrices $W_k$ with update rule $w_{ij}^{[k]} = w_{ij}^{[k-1]} \vee (w_{ik}^{[k-1]} \wedge w_{kj}^{[k-1]})$.

### Section 9.5: Equivalence Relations
*See [[09.5 Equivalence Relations]]*

- **Equivalence Class Properties:** Let $R$ be an equivalence relation on a set $A$. The following statements are equivalent for elements $a, b \in A$:
  1. $a R b$
  2. $[a] = [b]$
  3. $[a] \cap [b] \neq \emptyset$
- **Equivalence Relations and Partitions Theorem:** The equivalence classes of an equivalence relation on a set $S$ form a partition of $S$. Conversely, given a partition $\{A_i \mid i \in I\}$ of $S$, there is an equivalence relation $R$ having the sets $A_i$ as its equivalence classes.

### Section 9.6: Partial Orderings
*See [[09.6 Partial Orderings]]*

- **The Principle of Well-Ordered Induction:** If $S$ is a well-ordered set and for every $y \in S$, $P(x)$ being true for all $x \prec y$ implies $P(y)$ is true, then $P(x)$ is true for all $x \in S$.
- **Existence of Minimal Elements:** Every finite nonempty poset has at least one minimal element.
- **Topological Sorting Algorithm:** Every finite nonempty poset can be linearized into a compatible total ordering by repeatedly selecting and removing a minimal element.
