## NM-1.2 — Mathematical Modeling and Engineering Problem Solving

### 📝 Definitions for NM-1.2

> **Mathematical model:** A set of equations that captures the behaviour of a real system, with stated simplifications. A model is not a copy of reality. It is a deliberate simplification. Choosing what to leave out is the engineering judgement.

### Theory

Engineering problems are too large to test directly. A model gives numbers before the hardware exists.

**Why modeling matters:**
1. A real system cannot be rebuilt on demand, but its model can be run as many times as needed.
2. Trials on the model are cheap. One run costs nothing compared with one prototype.
3. The model shows which quantity drives the result. Effort goes where it helps.
4. It gives numbers before the hardware exists, which supports a budget.

**How a problem becomes a model (five steps):**
1. List the quantities of interest. Give each a symbol and a unit.
2. Write the physics or statistics that relates them.
3. State the simplifications accepted. A model is wrong somewhere by design. Saying where is part of the answer.
4. Fix the input data and solve.
5. Compare the answer against a measurement already trusted. Correct the model if it is off.

### 🏆 Golden Questions for NM-1.2

#### 🎯 Explain the importance of mathematical modeling in engineering problem solving; provide examples.
> **Appeared:** 2024 Q.1(a) — (03 marks)

**Full Answer:**

Engineering problems are too large to test directly. A model is a set of equations that captures the behaviour that matters and drops the rest.

**Why it matters:**
1. A real system cannot be rebuilt or damaged on demand, but its model can be run as many times as needed.
2. Trials on the model are cheap. One run costs nothing compared with one prototype.
3. The model shows which quantity drives the result, so effort goes where it helps.
4. It gives numbers before the hardware exists, which supports a budget.

**How a problem becomes a model:**
1. List the quantities of interest and give each a symbol and a unit.
2. Write the physics, or the statistics, that relates them.
3. State the simplifications accepted. A model is wrong somewhere by design.
4. Fix the input data and solve.
5. Compare the answer against a measurement already trusted, then correct the model if it is off.

**Worked example.** A reactor's coolant temperature must be known every second, and the thermocouple is slow. Model the exponential decay of the sensor reading: store the last reading $T_{n-1}$, then

$$
T_n = T_{n-1} + (T_\infty - T_{n-1})e^{-\Delta t/\tau}
$$

with $\tau$ the sensor time constant. The thermocouple is the real system, and the first-order lag is the model. For $\Delta t \ll \tau$ this reduces to $T_n - T_{n-1} \approx (\Delta t/\tau)(T_\infty - T_{n-1})$, the linear formula used in a circuit.

$$
\boxed{\text{Model} = \text{physics written as equations, with stated limits}}
$$

#### 🎯 CT-01 Q.1(a): Develop a mathematical model representing the temperature estimation problem and identify the assumptions made.
> **Appeared:** CT-01 Q.1(a)

**Full Answer:**

Let $T(t)$ be the reactor's temperature at time $t$. For a well-mixed reactor the energy balance per unit mass is

$$
C_p \frac{dT}{dt} = Q(t) - hA\bigl(T - T_a(t)\bigr)
$$

where $C_p$ is the specific heat capacity, $Q(t)$ the heat input from the reaction, $h$ the convective heat-transfer coefficient, $A$ the internal surface area, and $T_a(t)$ the ambient or coolant temperature. This is a first-order initial value problem in $T$.

The software evaluates the exponential with the first four non-zero Taylor terms:

$$
e^z \approx 1 + z + \frac{z^2}{2!} + \frac{z^3}{3!}
$$

with remainder $\frac{z^4 e^{\xi}}{4!}$ for some $\xi \in (0, z)$.

**The assumptions:**
1. The reactor is well mixed. Temperature is uniform throughout the volume.
2. $C_p$, $h$, and $A$ are constant. Properties are treated as independent of temperature.
3. $Q(t)$ is known. The heat release is either measured or computed from stoichiometry.
4. Heat loss is linear in the temperature difference (Newton's law of cooling).
5. Ambient conditions are measured, not predicted.
6. No heat is generated by the measurement or by stirring.

## NM-1.3 — Accuracy, Precision & Significant Digits

### 📝 Definitions for NM-1.3

> **Significant digits:** "The digits that are used to express a number are called significant digits or significant figures." — Jain, §1.3 (p. 7)

> **Rounding off:** "To round-off a number to $n$ significant digits, discard all digits to the right of the $n$th digit, and if this discarded number is (a) less than half a unit in the $n$th place, leave the $n$th digit unaltered; (b) greater than half a unit in the $n$th place, increase the $n$th digit by unity; (c) exactly half a unit in the $n$th place, increase the $n$th digit by unity if it is odd; otherwise, leave it unchanged." — Jain, §1.3 (p. 7)

> **Accuracy:** Closeness of a computed or measured value to the *true* value. Quantitatively: $1 - |\text{relative error}|$.

> **Precision:** Closeness of repeated measurements to *one another*. It says nothing about whether the cluster is near the true value.

### Theory

**Accuracy vs. Precision:** Think of four target-shooting groups:
- High accuracy, high precision: tight cluster on bullseye.
- High accuracy, low precision: spread out but centered on bullseye.
- Low accuracy, high precision: tight cluster, off the bullseye.
- Low accuracy, low precision: spread out and off the bullseye.

Accuracy is distance from the bullseye. Precision is how tightly the shots cluster. A repeatability cluster that sits $2\%$ off is a situation where averaging will not help.

**Significant digits rules:**
- $3.1416$, $0.66667$, $4.0687$ each have five significant digits.
- $0.00023$ has only two significant digits ($2$ and $3$). Leading zeros only fix the decimal position.
- $0.00145$, $0.000145$, and $0.0000145$ all have three significant digits.

**The rounding rule when the discarded digit is exactly $5$:** round to even. This is the unbiased rule and it is worth knowing by name.

### Procedure for Adding Numbers of Different Accuracies

From Jain §1.3: When adding numbers of different absolute accuracies:

1. **Isolate** the number with the greatest absolute error.
2. **Round off** all other numbers retaining one digit more than the isolated number.
3. **Add up**.
4. **Round off** the sum by discarding one digit.

### 🏆 Golden Questions for NM-1.3

#### 🎯 Define the following terms with example: (i) Accuracy (ii) Precision (iii) truncation error (iv) round off error.
> **Appeared:** 2017 Q.1(b) — (04 marks)

**Full Answer:**

**Accuracy** is the closeness of a computed or measured value to the true value. Quantitatively it is $1 - |{\text{relative error}}|$, or, as a percentage, $100(1 - |{\text{relative error}}|)$. Accuracy is an absolute measure: it does not depend on how far apart repeated readings are.

*Example.* A voltmeter reads $9.8\ \text{V}$ across a true $10\ \text{V}$ supply. The relative error is $-0.02$, so the accuracy is $98\%$.

**Precision** is the closeness of repeated measurements of the same quantity to one another. It quantifies the spread of the readings, and is expressed as $100(1 - |{\text{relative spread}}|)$ or through the variance of the readings. Precision is a relative measure: it says nothing about whether the cluster of readings is near the true value.

*Example.* Five readings of the same supply give $9.8, 9.9, 9.8, 9.9, 9.8$. The spread is $0.1$ in $9.8$, about $1\%$, so the readings are $99\%$ precise. They are still only $98\%$ accurate, because the whole cluster sits low.

**Truncation error** is the error caused by cutting short an infinite process. Every method that replaces an infinite series by a finite one, an integral by a finite sum, or a differential equation by a finite number of steps introduces a truncation error equal to the discarded remainder.

*Example.* $\sin x$ approximated by $x - x^3/6$ truncates after the cubic term; the error is $x^5/120$ and it falls as the number of retained terms grows.

**Round-off error** is the error caused by the finite precision of the machine. Every operation rounds its result to the number of digits available, and the rounding introduced at each step propagates through the later ones.

*Example.* On a machine holding six significant digits, $1/3$ is stored as $0.333333$. Adding it three times gives $0.999999$, not $1$, and the $10^{-6}$ shortfall is round-off error.

#### 🎯 Sum all the numbers: 0.1532, 15.45, 0.000354, 305.4, 8.12, 143.3 where all given digits are correct.
> **Appeared:** 2017 Q.3(a) — (03 marks)

**Full Answer:**

**Approach 1: Direct arithmetic sum.**

$$
0.1532 + 15.45 + 0.000354 + 305.4 + 8.12 + 143.3 = 472.423554
$$

$$
\boxed{472.423554}
$$

**Approach 2: Standard textbook procedure for approximate numbers (Sastry / Jain).** The phrase "all given digits are correct" means each number is an approximate value rounded to its specified decimal places. The absolute error does not exceed $0.5 \times 10^{-m}$ in each case.

1. **Identify numbers with the largest absolute error:** $305.4$ and $143.3$ (1 decimal place). Absolute error in each is $E_A = 0.05$.
2. **Round all other numbers to 2 decimal places:**
   - $0.1532 \to 0.15$
   - $15.45 \to 15.45$
   - $0.000354 \to 0.00$
   - $8.12 \to 8.12$
3. **Compute the sum:** $S = 305.4 + 143.3 + 0.15 + 15.45 + 0.00 + 8.12 = 472.42$
4. **Round off by discarding one digit:** $S \approx 472.4$
5. **Error bound:** $E_A = 2(0.05) + 4(0.005) = 0.10 + 0.02 = 0.12$. Adding rounding error ($0.01$) gives total $0.13$. So $S = 472.42 \pm 0.13$.

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Common Mistakes

1. **Accuracy ≠ Precision.** These are not synonyms. Accuracy is about the true value. Precision is about spread. Both examples must be different to show this.
2. **Truncation error shrinks with refinement, round-off error grows.** Do not merge them. They are different error types with opposite behaviour.
3. **For summation of approximate numbers**, always identify the least precise number first and round others to one extra decimal place.
4. **Mathematical modeling questions want five steps.** List quantities, write physics, state simplifications, solve, validate. The simplification step is where marks are awarded or lost.
5. **Present both the direct sum and the textbook procedure** for summation questions. This covers all possible examiner interpretations.
