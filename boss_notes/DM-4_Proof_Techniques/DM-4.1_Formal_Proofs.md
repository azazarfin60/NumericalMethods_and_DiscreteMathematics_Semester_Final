# DM-4.1: Structure of Formal Proofs

> ⚫ **Never examined.** This note exists for reading flow and syllabus completeness. Skim it in 2 minutes to connect the ideas, but do not spend revision time memorizing it.

## What This Is

A mathematical proof is a valid argument that establishes the truth of a statement beyond any doubt. It begins with axioms, definitions, and premises, then applies rules of inference step by step to reach the conclusion (Rosen §1.7).

### Key Definitions (from Rosen §1.7)

> **Theorem:** "A statement that can be shown to be true."
>
> **In simple words:** A major mathematical statement that has been proven true using rigorous logic.

> **Axiom (Postulate):** "A statement that is assumed to be true without proof."
>
> **In simple words:** An agreed starting rule of mathematics that we accept as given, such as basic properties of real numbers.

> **Lemma:** "A less important theorem that is helpful in the proof of other results."
>
> **In simple words:** A stepping-stone fact proven on the way to a larger theorem.

> **Corollary:** "A theorem that can be established directly from a theorem that has been proved."
>
> **In simple words:** A quick bonus result that falls out immediately once the main theorem is established.

> **Conjecture:** "A statement that is being proposed to be a true statement, usually on the basis of some partial evidence."
>
> **In simple words:** An educated guess that appears true but still needs a proof (or a counterexample).

---

## Key Idea: The Proof Chain

A formal proof links statements in an unbroken logical chain:
1. **Premises and Axioms:** Start with known facts and clear definitions.
2. **Valid Deductions:** Use inference rules (such as Modus Ponens) so each new line is guaranteed true.
3. **Conclusion:** End at the target statement.
