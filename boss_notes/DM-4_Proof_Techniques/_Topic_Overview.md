## 📖 How to Read This Topic

Proof techniques provide the logical structure for mathematical certainty. In discrete mathematics, you do not just calculate numerical outputs; you establish why statements must hold true under all conditions.

In RUET semester final exams, examiners have never asked standalone theoretical questions on proof techniques (such as "Define proof by contradiction" or "State the principle of induction"). However, proof logic appears in embedded form inside number theory questions (such as parity and divisibility proofs under DM-5.9).

Skim through this folder in order:
1. **DM-4.1 (Formal Proofs):** The vocabulary of mathematics: axioms, definitions, theorems, lemmas, corollaries, and conjectures.
2. **DM-4.2 (Direct Proofs):** The direct route: assume premise $p$, apply definitions and algebra, and arrive at conclusion $q$.
3. **DM-4.3 (Proof by Contradiction):** Indirect reasoning: assume the claim is false, deduce an impossible clash, and conclude the original claim must hold.
4. **DM-4.4 (Proof by Contraposition):** Indirect conditional reasoning: prove $p \to q$ by proving its logically equivalent contrapositive $\neg q \to \neg p$.
5. **DM-4.5 (Mathematical Induction):** The chain-reaction proof for all positive integers: check base case $P(1)$, prove the inductive step $P(k) \to P(k+1)$, and conclude $\forall n P(n)$.

**Suggested reading time:** ~15 minutes for the entire folder.
