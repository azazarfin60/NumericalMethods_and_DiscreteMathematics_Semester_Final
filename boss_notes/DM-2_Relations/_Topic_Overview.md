## 📖 How to Read This Topic

Relations build on Cartesian products of sets. They describe how elements from one set link to elements in another set, or back to themselves.

In computer science, relations form the foundation of relational databases, graph models, and partial task schedules.

Read the subtopics in this sequence:
- **DM-2.1** introduces the core properties: reflexive, symmetric, antisymmetric, and transitive relations.
- **DM-2.2** presents the two standard visual tools: zero-one matrices and directed graphs (digraphs).
- **DM-2.3** examines partial orders, posets, and Hasse diagrams for task scheduling.
- **DM-2.4** covers equivalence relations, equivalence classes, and set partitions.

**Suggested reading time:** ~15 minutes for the entire topic folder.

Relations have never appeared on any RUET semester final exam from 2017 through 2024. In Section B (introduced in 2022), exam questions focus on:

Because relations are listed in the official ECE 2213 syllabus, examiners could test basic definitions or matrix representations in a short sub-question (3 to 4 marks).

**Most likely hypothetical question shapes:**
- State whether a given relation is an equivalence relation or a partial order.
- Represent a small finite relation with a zero-one matrix or a digraph.
- Draw the Hasse diagram for a small poset (such as the divides relation on a finite set).
