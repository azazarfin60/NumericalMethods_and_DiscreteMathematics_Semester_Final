# NM-10.1: PDE Classification and Theory

> 🟠 **Priority:** Secondary | **Tested:** 2020 Q.8(a) | **Typical marks:** 4 to 5

## What This Is (and Why It Matters)

In ODEs, our unknown function depends on only one variable, such as time $t$. But real electrical engineering problems rarely live in a single dimension. Electrostatic potentials, electromagnetic waves, and thermal distributions in microchips vary across space ($x, y, z$) and time ($t$). Equations relating these multivariable rates of change are called Partial Differential Equations (PDEs).

Before you attempt to solve a PDE numerically, you have to know what kind of creature it is. Physical behavior dictates numerical behavior. For instance, waves propagate along sharp wavefronts, heat diffuses smoothly over time, and steady-state electric fields settle into equilibrium governed entirely by surrounding boundaries. 

The exam tests whether you can classify an arbitrary second-order PDE into its correct mathematical family, and whether you can describe how an iterative scheme marches towards the solution.

---

## 📝 Key Definitions

> **Partial Differential Equation (PDE):** "An equation involving partial derivatives of an unknown function of two or more independent variables is called a partial differential equation." -- Jain, §9.1 (p. 342)
>
> **In simple words:** It is an equation where the rate of change of an unknown quantity depends on multiple inputs at the same time, like position $x$ and time $t$.

> **Classification of Second-Order PDEs:** "The general second order linear partial differential equation $A u_{xx} + B u_{xy} + C u_{yy} + D u_x + E u_y + Fu = G$ is said to be elliptic, parabolic or hyperbolic according as the discriminant $\Delta = B^2 - 4AC < 0$, $\Delta = 0$, or $\Delta > 0$." -- Jain, §9.1 (p. 343)
>
> **In simple words:** Just like the discriminant in high-school quadratic formulas sorts conic sections into ellipses, parabolas, and hyperbolas, calculating $B^2 - 4AC$ tells you immediately whether a PDE represents a steady-state field, a smooth diffusion process, or a travelling wave.

> **Well-Posed Problem:** "In partial differential equations, the form of the equation is always associated with a particular type of boundary conditions. In this case, the problem is said to be well-defined (or well-posed). A problem is well-posed if a unique solution exists and depends continuously on the initial and boundary data." -- Jain, §9.1 (p. 343)
>
> **In simple words:** A problem is well-posed when it gives you just enough physics (conditions) to find exactly one stable, realistic answer. Give too few conditions, and infinite answers exist; give conflicting conditions, and no answer exists.

## The Core Idea

### The General Linear Second-Order PDE

Any linear second-order partial differential equation in two spatial dimensions can be written in the standard form:

$$
A \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2} + B \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x \partial y} + C \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2} + D \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + E \frac{\partial u}{\partial y} + F u = G
$$

In compact subscript notation, this is:

$$
A u_{xx} + B u_{xy} + C u_{yy} + D u_x + E u_y + F u = G
$$

Here the coefficients $A, B, C, D, E, F$ and $G$ can be constants or functions of $x$ and $y$. The classification depends entirely on the coefficients of the highest (second-order) derivative terms: $A$, $B$, and $C$.

### The Discriminant Test

We calculate the discriminant:

$$
\Delta = B^2 - 4AC
$$

*(Note: If the mixed derivative is written with a factor of 2 as $2B u_{xy}$, the discriminant simplifies to $B^2 - AC$. The signs and rules remain identical).*

| Sign of Discriminant | Mathematical Type | Physical Nature | Canonical Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\Delta < 0$ | **Elliptic** | Steady state, equilibrium, potential fields | Laplace equation: $u_{xx} + u_{yy} = 0$<br>Poisson equation: $u_{xx} + u_{yy} = -f(x,y)$ |
| $\Delta = 0$ | **Parabolic** | Diffusion, dissipative evolution in time | Heat equation: $u_t = \alpha (u_{xx} + u_{yy})$ |
| $\Delta > 0$ | **Hyperbolic** | Wave propagation, vibrations, no dissipation | Wave equation: $u_{tt} = c^2 (u_{xx} + u_{yy})$ |

### The Four Classical Equations

You must memorize the equations and physical meanings of all four classical types:

1. **Laplace Equation (Elliptic):**
   $$
   \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2} = 0 \quad (\nabla^2 u = 0)
   $$
   *Physical meaning:* Steady-state temperature in a plate, electrostatic potential in charge-free space, irrotational fluid flow. There is no time variable; the solution inside is locked into place entirely by fixed values along the boundaries (Dirichlet problem).

2. **Poisson Equation (Elliptic):**
   $$
   \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2} = -f(x,y) \quad (\nabla^2 u = -f(x,y))
   $$
   *Physical meaning:* Laplace equation with an internal generation source, such as electrostatic potential in regions containing space charge density $\rho$, or steady-state heat conduction with internal resistive heating.

3. **Heat / Diffusion Equation (Parabolic):**
   $$
   \frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = \alpha \left(\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2}\right) \quad \left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = \alpha \nabla^2 u\right)
   $$
   *Physical meaning:* Transient heat conduction through a solid medium, diffusion of charge carriers in semiconductors, spread of pollutants. Here $\alpha = \frac{k}{\rho c}$ is thermal diffusivity. It requires an initial spatial distribution $u(x,y,0)$ plus edge boundary conditions.

4. **Wave Equation (Hyperbolic):**
   $$
   \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2} = c^2 \left(\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2}\right) \quad \left(\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2} = c^2 \nabla^2 u\right)
   $$
   *Physical meaning:* Vibrating mechanical strings and membranes, acoustic pressure waves, electromagnetic waves traveling through waveguides. Here $c$ is the wave propagation speed. It requires two initial conditions (initial shape $u(x,0)$ and initial velocity $u_t(x,0)$) plus edge boundary conditions.

## Numerical Iterative Solving Process

When the exam asks you to "discuss briefly the solving process of any of the equations using numerical iterative technique" (as in 2020 Q.8(a)), the classic demonstration is the **time-dependent heat equation** or the **steady-state elliptic equation**. Let us walk through the heat equation, which shows both time marching and spatial averaging.

### Step 1: Discretize the Domain
Divide the physical domain into a regular spatial mesh of spacing $h$ in both $x$ and $y$ directions, and slice time into discrete time steps $\Delta t$:
- Spatial coordinates: $x_i = i h$, $y_j = j h$
- Temporal coordinates: $t_n = n \Delta t$
- Grid value: $u_{i,j}^n \approx u(x_i, y_j, t_n)$

### Step 2: Replace Derivatives with Finite Differences
Approximate the time derivative with a forward difference:
$$
\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} \approx \frac{u_{i,j}^{n+1} - u_{i,j}^n}{\Delta t}
$$
Approximate second-order spatial derivatives with central differences:
$$
\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2} \approx \frac{u_{i+1,j}^n - 2u_{i,j}^n + u_{i-1,j}^n}{h^2}
$$
$$
\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2} \approx \frac{u_{i,j+1}^n - 2u_{i,j}^n + u_{i,j-1}^n}{h^2}
$$

### Step 3: Formulate the Update Equation
Substitute these approximations into $\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = \alpha (u_{xx} + u_{yy})$:

$$
\frac{u_{i,j}^{n+1} - u_{i,j}^n}{\Delta t} = \alpha \left[\frac{u_{i+1,j}^n + u_{i-1,j}^n + u_{i,j+1}^n + u_{i,j-1}^n - 4u_{i,j}^n}{h^2}\right]
$$

Define the dimensionless mesh ratio:
$$
\lambda = \frac{\alpha \Delta t}{h^2}
$$

Rearranging gives the explicit time-marching formula:

$$
u_{i,j}^{n+1} = \lambda \left(u_{i+1,j}^n + u_{i-1,j}^n + u_{i,j+1}^n + u_{i,j-1}^n\right) + (1 - 4\lambda) u_{i,j}^n
$$

Notice what this formula means: the temperature at the new time step is a **weighted average** of the four surrounding spatial neighbors plus the old center point.

### Step 4: Numerical Stability Condition
For the explicit scheme to be numerically stable, the weight on the center point $(1 - 4\lambda)$ cannot be negative. This gives the famous stability criterion in 2D:

$$
\lambda = \frac{\alpha \Delta t}{h^2} \le \frac{1}{4}
$$

If your time step is chosen too large such that $\Delta t > \frac{h^2}{4\alpha}$, errors oscillate wildly and blow up to infinity.

### Alternative Time Schemes

| Method | Stability Limit | Cost per Step | Accuracy in Time | When to Use |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Explicit (Forward Euler)** | Needs $\lambda \le \frac{1}{4}$ | Very low (direct formula) | $O(\Delta t)$ | Short simulation runs, simple coding |
| **Implicit (Backward Euler)** | Unconditionally stable | Requires solving linear system | $O(\Delta t)$ | Stiff problems, large time steps |
| **Crank-Nicolson** | Unconditionally stable | Requires solving linear system | $O((\Delta t)^2)$ | High accuracy requirements |

### The Elliptic Connection (Liebmann's Method)
What if the problem is steady-state (elliptic), so $\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = 0$? 
Then time vanishes. Instead of marching forward in time $t$, we march the **iteration count $k$**!

Setting $\lambda = \frac{1}{4}$ turns the explicit update equation into:

$$
u_{i,j}^{(k+1)} = \frac{1}{4}\left(u_{i+1,j}^{(k)} + u_{i-1,j}^{(k)} + u_{i,j+1}^{(k)} + u_{i,j-1}^{(k)}\right)
$$

Repeatedly averaging the four neighbors until the numbers stop changing is the foundation of **Liebmann's method**.

## 🏆 Past Exam Questions

### 2020 Q.8(a): Four Types of PDEs and Numerical Iterative Technique -- [05 marks]

**Question:** Write 4 types of partial differential equations. Discuss briefly the solving process of any of the equations using numerical iterative technique.

**Let's solve this step by step.**

#### Part 1: State the Four Classical Types of PDEs
A partial differential equation involves partial derivatives with respect to two or more independent variables. The four classical types are:

1. **Laplace equation:** No time or reaction term, steady state.
   $$
   \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2} = 0
   $$
   *Physical meaning:* Steady-state heat conduction, electrostatics, incompressible irrotational flow, torsional rigidity. Boundaries are prescribed values of $u$ on the edges of a region.

2. **Poisson equation:** Laplace with a source term.
   $$
   \nabla^2 u = -f(x,y)
   $$
   *Physical meaning:* Steady heat conduction with internal heat generation, electrostatics with a charge density, potential flow with sources. Same boundary condition type as Laplace.

3. **Wave equation:** Time-dependent, second order in both space and time.
   $$
   \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2} = c^2\nabla^2 u
   $$
   *Physical meaning:* Vibrating string, sound in air, water waves, electromagnetic waves. Here $c$ is the wave speed. Needs an initial condition $u(x,0) = f(x)$, $\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(x,0) = g(x)$, plus boundary conditions.

4. **Heat equation (diffusion equation):** Time-dependent, second order in space, first order in time.
   $$
   \frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = \alpha\nabla^2 u
   $$
   *Physical meaning:* Heat conduction in a solid, diffusion of a solute, spread of an epidemic. Here $\alpha = \frac{k}{\rho c}$ is thermal diffusivity. Needs an initial condition $u(x,y,0) = f(x,y)$ plus boundary conditions.

#### Part 2: The Deciding Discriminant Test
For a general linear second-order PDE $A u_{xx} + 2B u_{xy} + C u_{yy} + \cdots = 0$, compute the discriminant $B^2 - AC$:

| Sign of $B^2 - AC$ | Type | Example |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| $< 0$ | **Elliptic** | Laplace, Poisson |
| $= 0$ | **Parabolic** | Heat equation |
| $> 0$ | **Hyperbolic** | Wave equation |

#### Part 3: Iterative Solving Process for the Heat Equation

$$
\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = \alpha\left(\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2}\right)
$$

1. **Discretize the grid:** Divide the region into a mesh of spacing $h$ in space and $\Delta t$ in time. The stability condition in two dimensions is:
   $$
   \alpha \frac{\Delta t}{h^2} \le \frac{1}{4}
   $$

2. **Replace derivatives with finite differences:** Approximate time with forward difference and spatial derivatives with central differences:
   $$
   \frac{u^{n+1}_{i,j} - u^n_{i,j}}{\Delta t} = \alpha \frac{u^n_{i+1,j} - 2u^n_{i,j} + u^n_{i-1,j}}{h^2}
   $$

3. **Rearrange into the update formula:** Setting $\lambda = \frac{\alpha \Delta t}{h^2}$:
   $$
   u^{n+1}_{i,j} = \lambda u^n_{i+1,j} + \lambda u^n_{i-1,j} + \lambda u^n_{i,j+1} + \lambda u^n_{i,j-1} + (1 - 4\lambda)u^n_{i,j}
   $$
   Each new value is a weighted average of the four neighbors plus the old center. The weights are all non-negative as long as $\lambda \le \frac{1}{4}$.

4. **March in time:** The scheme is explicit, so each time level follows directly from the previous one. Start from the initial condition, apply boundary values at every step, and advance until reaching the desired time.

5. **For elliptic equations (Laplace and Poisson):** Time does not appear, so instead of marching time, we march the iteration count. Replacing each interior node by the average of its four neighbors and repeating is exactly the **Liebmann method**.

$$
\boxed{\text{Laplace, Poisson, wave, heat; the elliptic pair is solved by repeated averaging of the four neighbours}}
$$

## ⚡ Quick Review

**The topic in one sentence:** Second-order PDEs are classified by $B^2 - 4AC$ into elliptic (steady equilibrium), parabolic (time diffusion), or hyperbolic (travelling waves).

**The formulas you must memorize:**
- General PDE: $A u_{xx} + B u_{xy} + C u_{yy} + D u_x + E u_y + Fu = G$
- Discriminant test: $\Delta = B^2 - 4AC$ (or $B^2 - AC$ if written $2B u_{xy}$)
- Stability limit for explicit 2D heat equation: $\lambda = \frac{\alpha \Delta t}{h^2} \le \frac{1}{4}$

**The most common exam mistake:** Mixing up the stability limit ($\le \frac{1}{4}$ for 2D vs $\le \frac{1}{2}$ for 1D) or forgetting the discriminant criteria.

**Time estimate in exam:** 8 to 10 minutes for a complete theory question.
