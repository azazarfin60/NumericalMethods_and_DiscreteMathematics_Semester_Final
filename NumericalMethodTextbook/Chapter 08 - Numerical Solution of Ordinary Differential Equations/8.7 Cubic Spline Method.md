## 8.7 CUBIC SPLINE METHOD

The governing equations of a cubic spline have been discussed in detail in Section 5.2, where the cubic spline function has been obtained in terms of its second derivatives, $M_i$. In certain applications, e.g. the solution of initial-value problems, it would be convenient to use the governing equations in terms of its first derivatives, i.e., $m_i$. Using Hermite’s interpolation formula (see Section 3.9.3), it would not be difficult to derive the following formula for the cubic spline $s(x)$ in $x_{i-1} \le x \le x_i$ in terms of its first derivatives $s'(x_i) = m_i$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
s(x) &= m_{i-1} \frac{(x_i - x)^2(x - x_{i-1})}{h^2} - m_i \frac{(x - x_{i-1})^2(x_i - x)}{h^2} \\
&\quad + y_{i-1} \frac{(x_i - x)^2[2(x - x_{i-1}) + h]}{h^3} + y_i \frac{(x - x_{i-1})^2[2(x_i - x) + h]}{h^3}, \tag{8.35}
\end{aligned}
$$
where $h = x_i - x_{i-1}$. Differentiating Eq. (8.35) with respect to $x$ and simplifying, we obtain
$$
\begin{aligned}
s'(x) &= \frac{m_{i-1}}{h^2}(x_i - x)(2x_{i-1} + x_i - 3x) - \frac{m_i}{h^2}(x - x_{i-1})(x_{i-1} + 2x_i - 3x) \\
&\quad + \frac{6(y_i - y_{i-1})}{h^3}(x - x_{i-1})(x_i - x). \tag{8.36}
\end{aligned}
$$
Again,
$$
\begin{aligned}
s''(x) &= -\frac{2m_{i-1}}{h^2}(x_{i-1} + 2x_i - 3x) - \frac{2m_i}{h^2}(2x_{i-1} + x_i - 3x) \\
&\quad + \frac{6(y_i - y_{i-1})}{h^3}(x_{i-1} + x_i - 2x), \tag{8.37}
\end{aligned}
$$

<!-- Page 322 -->

which gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
s''(x_i) &= \frac{2m_{i-1}}{h} + \frac{4m_i}{h} - \frac{6}{h^2}(y_i - y_{i-1}) \\
&= \frac{2m_{i-1}}{h} + \frac{4m_i}{h} - \frac{6}{h^2}(s_i - s_{i-1}). \tag{8.38}
\end{aligned}
$$
If we now consider the initial-value problem
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = f(x, y) \tag{8.39a}$$
and
$$y(x_0) = y_0 \tag{8.39b}$$
then from Eq. (8.39a), we obtain
$$\frac{d^2 y}{dx^2} = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial f}{\partial y} \frac{dy}{dx},$$
or
$$
\begin{aligned}
y''(x_i) &= f_x(x_i, y_i) + f_y(x_i, y_i) f(x_i, y_i) \\
&= f_x(x_i, s_i) + f_y(x_i, s_i) f(x_i, s_i). \tag{8.40}
\end{aligned}
$$
Equating Eqs. (8.38) and (8.40), we obtain
$$\frac{2m_{i-1}}{h} + \frac{4m_i}{h} - \frac{6}{h^2}(s_i - s_{i-1}) = f_x(x_i, s_i) + f_y(x_i, s_i) f(x_i, s_i) \tag{8.41}$$
from which $s_i$ can be computed. Substitution in Eq. (8.35) gives the required solution.

The following example demonstrates the usefulness of the spline method.

> [!NOTE] EXAMPLE 8.14
> We consider again the initial-value problem defined by
> $$y' = 3x + \frac{1}{2} y, \qquad y(0) = 1, \tag{i}$$
> whose exact solution is given by
> $$y = 13e^{x/2} - 6x - 12 \tag{ii}$$
> We take, for simplicity, $n = 2$, i.e. $h = 0.5$ and compute the value of $y(0.5)$. Here $f(x, y) = 3x + y/2$ and therefore we have $f_x = 3$ and $f_y = 1/2$. Also,
> $$f(x_i, s_i) = 3x_i + \frac{1}{2} s_i.$$
> 
> <!-- Page 323 -->
> 
> Hence, Eq. (8.41) gives
> $$4m_0 + 8m_1 - 24(s_1 - s_0) = 3 + \frac{1}{2}\left( \frac{3}{2} + \frac{1}{2} s_1 \right)$$
> and
> $$4m_1 + 8m_2 - 24(s_2 - s_1) = 3 + \frac{1}{2}\left( 3 + \frac{1}{2} s_2 \right)$$
> Since $m_0 = 1/2$, $m_1 = 3/2 + s_1/2$ and $m_2 = 3 + s_2/2$, the above equations give on simplification
> $$s_1 = 1.691358 \quad \text{and} \quad s_2 = 3.430879.$$
> The errors in these solutions are given by $0.000972$ and $0.002497$, respectively. It can be shown that, under certain conditions, the spline method gives $\mathcal{O}(h^4)$ convergence and compares well with the multi-step Milne’s method. For details, the reader is referred to Patricio [1978].
