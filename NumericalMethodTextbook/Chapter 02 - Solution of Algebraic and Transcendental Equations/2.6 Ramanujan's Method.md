## 2.6 RAMANUJAN'S METHOD

Srinivasa Ramanujan (1887–1920) described an iterative procedure\* to determine the *smallest* root of the equation
$$f(x) = 0, \tag{2.1}$$
where $f(x)$ is of the form
$$f(x) = 1 - (a_1 x + a_2 x^2 + a_3 x^3 + \cdots) \tag{2.32}$$
To explain the method of procedure, we consider the quadratic equation
$$f(x) = a_0 x^2 + a_1 x + a_2 = 0,$$
with the roots $x_1$ and $x_2$, such that $|x_1| < |x_2|$. Then the equation defined by
$$\phi(x) = a_2 x^2 + a_1 x + a_0 = 0$$
$$\Rightarrow \qquad 1 + \frac{a_1}{a_0}x + \frac{a_2}{a_0}x^2 = 0$$
will have roots $\frac{1}{x_1}$ and $\frac{1}{x_2}$ such that $\frac{1}{|x_1|} > \frac{1}{|x_2|}$.
Now,
$$\frac{1}{\phi(x)} = \left(1 + \frac{a_1}{a_0}x + \frac{a_2}{a_0}x^2\right)^{-1}$$

<!-- Page 44 -->

can be written as
$$
\begin{aligned}
\left(1 + \frac{a_1}{a_0}x + \frac{a_2}{a_0}x^2\right)^{-1} &= \frac{k_1}{x - \frac{1}{x_1}} + \frac{k_2}{x - \frac{1}{x_2}} \\
&= \frac{-k_1 x_1}{1 - x x_1} + \frac{-k_2 x_2}{1 - x x_2} \\
&= -k_1 x_1 (1 - x x_1)^{-1} - k_2 x_2 (1 - x x_2)^{-1} \\
&= \sum_{i=0}^\infty b_i x^i, \quad \text{where } b_i = -\sum_{r=1}^2 k_r x_r^{i+1}
\end{aligned}
$$
Then,
$$\frac{b_{i-1}}{b_i} = \frac{k_1 x_1^i + k_2 x_2^i}{k_1 x_1^{i+1} + k_2 x_2^{i+1}} = \frac{\frac{k_1}{k_2}\left(\frac{x_1}{x_2}\right)^i + 1}{\frac{k_1}{k_2}\left(\frac{x_1}{x_2}\right)^{i+1} + 1} \cdot \frac{1}{x_2}$$
Since $\frac{x_1}{x_2} < 1$, it follows that
$$\lim_{i \to \infty} \frac{b_{i-1}}{b_i} = \frac{1}{x_2},$$
which is the smallest root. This is the basis of Ramanujan's method which is outlined below.

To find the smallest root of $f(x) = 0$, we consider $f(x)$ in the form
$$f(x) = 1 - (a_1 x + a_2 x^2 + a_3 x^3 + \cdots),$$
and then write
$$\left[1 - (a_1 x + a_2 x^2 + a_3 x^3 + \cdots)\right]^{-1} = b_1 + b_2 x + b_3 x^2 + \cdots \tag{2.33}$$
$$\Rightarrow \quad 1 + (a_1 x + a_2 x^2 + a_3 x^3 + \cdots) + (a_1 x + a_2 x^2 + a_3 x^3 + \cdots)^2 + \cdots$$
$$= b_1 + b_2 x + b_3 x^2 + \cdots \tag{2.34}$$
To find $b_i$, we equate coefficients of like powers of $x$ on both sides of Eq. (2.34). We then obtain
$$
\left.\begin{aligned}
b_1 &= 1 \\
b_2 &= a_1 = a_1 b_1, \quad \text{since } b_1 = 1 \\
b_3 &= a_2 + a_1^2 = a_2 b_1 + a_1 b_2, \quad \text{since } b_2 = a_1 \\
&\;\;\vdots \qquad \vdots \qquad \vdots \qquad\quad \vdots \\
b_k &= a_1 b_{k-1} + a_2 b_{k-2} + \cdots + a_{k-1} b_1 \\
&= a_{k-1} b_1 + a_{k-2} b_2 + \cdots + a_1 b_{k-1}
\end{aligned}\right\} \tag{2.35}
$$

The ratios $\frac{b_{i-1}}{b_i}$, called the *convergents*, approach, in the limit, the *smallest* root of $f(x) = 0$. The method is demonstrated in the following examples.

> [!NOTE] EXAMPLE 2.21
> Find the smallest root of the equation
> $$f(x) = x^3 - 9x^2 + 26x - 24 = 0$$
> 
> **Solution:**
> We have
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> f(x) &= 1 - \frac{26}{24}x + \frac{9}{24}x^2 - \frac{1}{24}x^3 \\
> &= 1 - \left(\frac{13}{12}x - \frac{3}{8}x^2 + \frac{1}{24}x^3\right)
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> Here
> $$a_1 = \frac{13}{12}, \quad a_2 = -\frac{3}{8}, \quad a_3 = \frac{1}{24}, \quad a_4 = a_5 = \cdots = 0$$
> Now,
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> b_1 &= 1 \\
> b_2 &= a_1 = \frac{13}{12} = 1.0833,
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> Therefore,
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \frac{b_1}{b_2} &= \frac{12}{13} = 0.923 \\
> b_3 &= a_1 b_2 + a_2 b_1 \\
> &= \frac{13}{12}(1.0833) - \frac{3}{8}(1) = 0.7986
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> Therefore,
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \frac{b_2}{b_3} &= \frac{1.0833}{0.7986} = 1.356 \\
> b_4 &= a_1 b_3 + a_2 b_2 + a_3 b_1 \\
> &= 1.0833(0.7986) + \left(-\frac{3}{8}\right)(1.0833) + \frac{1}{24}(1) \\
> &= 0.5007
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> Therefore,
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \frac{b_3}{b_4} &= 1.595 \\
> b_5 &= a_1 b_4 + a_2 b_3 + a_3 b_2 \\
> &= 1.0833(0.5007) + \left(-\frac{3}{8}\right)(0.7986) + \frac{1}{24}(1.0833) \\
> &= 0.2880
> \end{aligned}
> $$

Therefore,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{b_4}{b_5} &= \frac{0.5007}{0.2880} = 1.7382. \\
b_6 &= a_1 b_5 + a_2 b_4 + a_3 b_3 \\
&= 0.1575
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{b_5}{b_6} &= 1.8286. \\
b_7 &= 0.0835
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{b_6}{b_7} &= 1.8862. \\
b_8 &= 0.0434
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{b_7}{b_8} &= 1.9240. \\
b_9 &= 0.0223
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore,
$$\frac{b_8}{b_9} = 1.9462.$$
The roots of the given equation are $2$, $3$ and $4$ and it can be seen that the successive convergents approach the value $2$.
$\blacktriangleleft$

> [!NOTE] EXAMPLE 2.22
> Find a root of the equation $xe^x = 1$.
> 
> **Solution:**
> Let
> $$xe^x = 1 \tag{i}$$
> Expanding $e^x$ in ascending powers of $x$ and simplifying, we can rewrite Eq. (i) as
> $$1 = x + x^2 + \frac{x^3}{2} + \frac{x^4}{6} + \frac{x^5}{24} + \cdots \tag{ii}$$
> which is of the form of the right side of Eq. (2.32). Here,
> $$a_1 = 1, \quad a_2 = 1, \quad a_3 = \frac{1}{2}, \quad a_4 = \frac{1}{6}, \quad a_5 = \frac{1}{24}, \dots$$
> We then have
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> b_1 &= 1, \\
> b_2 &= a_2 = 1, \\
> b_3 &= a_1 b_2 + a_2 b_1 = 1 + 1 = 2 \\
> b_4 &= a_1 b_3 + a_2 b_2 + a_3 b_1 = 2 + 1 + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{7}{2},
> \end{aligned}
> $$

<!-- Page 47 -->

$$
\begin{aligned}
b_5 &= a_1 b_4 + a_2 b_3 + a_3 b_2 + a_4 b_1 \\
&= \frac{7}{2} + 2 + \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{6} \\
&= \frac{37}{6} = 6.1667, \\
b_6 &= \frac{261}{24} = 10.8750;
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence, we obtain
$$
\begin{aligned}
b_1/b_2 &= 1, \\
b_2/b_3 &= 0.5, \\
b_3/b_4 &= 0.5714, \\
b_4/b_5 &= 0.5676, \\
b_5/b_6 &= 0.5670.
\end{aligned}
$$
It can be seen that Newton's method (see Example 2.17) gives the value $0.5671433$ to this root.
$\blacktriangleleft$

> [!NOTE] EXAMPLE 2.23
> Find the smallest root, correct to 4 decimal places, of the equation
> $$f(x) = 3x - \cos x - 1 = 0.$$
> 
> **Solution:**
> We have
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> f(x) &= 1 - 3x + \cos x \\
> &= 1 - 3x + 1 - \frac{x^2}{2!} + \frac{x^4}{4!} - \frac{x^6}{6!} + \cdots \\
> &= 2 - 3x - \frac{x^2}{2!} + \frac{x^4}{4!} - \frac{x^6}{6!} + \cdots \\
> &= 2\left[1 - \frac{3}{2}x - \frac{x^2}{4} + \frac{x^4}{48} - \frac{x^6}{1440} + \frac{x^8}{80640} - \cdots\right]
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> Now, let
> $$1 - \left(\frac{3}{2}x + \frac{x^2}{4} - \frac{x^4}{48} + \frac{x^6}{1440} - \frac{x^8}{80640}\right)^{-1} = b_1 + b_2 x + b_3 x^2 + \cdots$$
> Here
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> a_1 &= \frac{3}{2}, & a_2 &= \frac{1}{4}, & a_3 &= 0, & a_4 &= -\frac{1}{48}, \\
> a_5 &= 0, & a_6 &= \frac{1}{1440}, & a_7 &= 0, & a_8 &= -\frac{1}{80640}, \dots
> \end{aligned}
> $$

we then obtain,
$$
\begin{aligned}
b_1 &= 1, & b_2 &= 1.5, \\
b_3 &= 2.5, & b_4 &= 4.125, \\
b_5 &= 6.79167, & b_6 &= 11.18750, \\
b_7 &= 18.42778, & b_8 &= 30.35365

> [!NOTE] EXAMPLE 2.24
> Using Ramanujan's method, find a real root of the equation
> $$1 - x + \frac{x^2}{(2!)^2} - \frac{x^3}{(3!)^2} + \frac{x^4}{(4!)^2} - \cdots = 0$$
> 
> **Solution:**
> Let
> $$1 - \left[x - \frac{x^2}{(2!)^2} + \frac{x^3}{(3!)^2} - \frac{x^4}{(4!)^2} + \cdots\right] = 0 \tag{i}$$
> we have
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> a_1 &= 1, & a_2 &= -\frac{1}{(2!)^2}, & a_3 &= \frac{1}{(3!)^2}, \\
> a_4 &= -\frac{1}{(4!)^2}, & a_5 &= \frac{1}{(5!)^2}, & a_6 &= -\frac{1}{(6!)^2}, \dots
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> Then we obtain
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> b_1 &= 1; & b_2 &= a_1 = 1; \\
> b_3 &= a_1 b_2 + a_2 b_1 = 1 - \frac{1}{(2!)^2} = \frac{3}{4}; \\
> b_4 &= a_1 b_3 + a_2 b_2 + a_3 b_1 \\
> &= \frac{3}{4} - \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{36} = \frac{19}{36}; \\
> b_5 &= \frac{211}{576}, \dots
> \end{aligned}
> $$

The successive convergents are:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{b_1}{b_2} &= 1; & \frac{b_2}{b_3} &= \frac{4}{3} = 1.3333\dots \\
\frac{b_3}{b_4} &= \frac{27}{19} = 1.4210\dots \\
